3D Scanning

Creality CR-Scan Otter

A structured-light 3D scanner that turns a real physical object into an editable, 3D-printable digital model.

Idea → finished product

  1. 1

    Prep your object. Matte, non-reflective, non-transparent surfaces scan best. Very shiny or clear objects may need a light dusting of scanning spray.

  2. 2

    Scan it. In Creality Scan software, start a new project, pick a scan mode (Geometry mode for objects with lots of shape detail, Marker mode for smooth/plain ones), and slowly rotate the object or move the scanner around it.

  3. 3

    Process the mesh. The software stitches your scan passes into a 3D mesh — clean up stray geometry and fill small holes here.

  4. 4

    Export & use. Export as STL/OBJ/PLY. From there you can 3D print it, edit it in CAD, or use it in a 3D project.

Quickstart

  • 1

    Connect the scanner to a laptop via USB-C (USB 3.0 port for best performance).

  • 2

    Open Creality Scan software and run a quick calibration if it's been a while since the last scan.

  • 3

    Place your object on the turntable, or plan to move the scanner around it by hand.

  • 4

    Start a new project, choose your scan mode, and begin scanning — watch the live preview to catch gaps early.

  • 5

    Once you've covered the whole object, generate the mesh and export your file.

⚠ Safety & who to ask

  • Handle the scanner gently — the optics inside are precise and not drop-proof.
  • Don't point the scanner directly into anyone's eyes; it uses infrared structured light.
  • Keep the lenses clean and dust-free — use a proper lens cloth, not your sleeve.
  • Store it in its case when not in use.

Scan not stitching properly? Ask staff before assuming the scanner is broken — it's usually a lighting or surface issue.
