Laser Cutting

Ruida-Controlled Laser Cutter

A CO2 laser cutter/engraver running a Ruida controller. Cuts and engraves wood, acrylic, cardboard, leather and fabric — never metals, PVC, or anything with chlorine in it.

Idea → finished product

  1. 1

    Make a vector file. Design in Inkscape (free), Adobe Illustrator, or LightBurn's own drawing tools. Cut lines and engrave areas should be on separate layers/colors.

  2. 2

    Load it into the control software. Import your file into RDWorks (comes free with the Ruida controller) or LightBurn (paid, but far friendlier — ask staff if it's installed).

  3. 3

    Set your material and test. Every material needs its own speed/power settings. Run a small test cut on scrap material first, never straight onto your final piece.

  4. 4

    Cut, then clear the fumes. Run the job with the extractor on, wait for it to fully finish, then remove your piece and clean up any offcuts and residue.

Quickstart

  • 1

    Turn on the machine, the exhaust/extractor, and the air assist compressor.

  • 2

    Home the laser head (usually automatic on power-up).

  • 3

    Load your material flat on the bed and set your focus height (many machines have an autofocus probe — use it).

  • 4

    Send your job from RDWorks/LightBurn, set speed/power for your material, and frame the job first to check positioning.

  • 5

    Close the lid, start the job, and stay in the room — never leave a running laser unattended.

⚠ Safety & who to ask

  • Never cut PVC, vinyl, or anything containing chlorine — it releases toxic gas and damages the machine.
  • Never leave a running laser unattended — stay in the room for the whole job.
  • Keep the extractor running throughout the whole cut, not just at the start.
  • Know where the fire extinguisher is before you start; keep a spray bottle of water nearby for small flare-ups.
  • Don't open the lid while the laser is firing.
  • If you haven't been shown the machine before, ask staff for a walkthrough — don't guess at settings.

Unsure about material or settings? Ask a makerspace staff member before you cut.
