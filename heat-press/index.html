Dye Sublimation

Perfect Laser Heat Press

Turns a printed sublimation design into a permanent, baked-in print on mugs, shirts, tiles and other coated blanks.

Idea → finished product

  1. 1

    Design your artwork in any image editor (Canva, Photoshop, GIMP). Mirror the image if you're printing text.

  2. 2

    Print on sublimation paper using the dedicated sublimation printer/ink — a normal printer's ink won't sublimate.

  3. 3

    Prep your blank. Only use sublimation-coated or high-polyester blanks (mugs, shirts, tiles) — sublimation doesn't work on cotton or uncoated surfaces.

  4. 4

    Press it. Tape the transfer face-down onto the blank, set the press to the right temperature/time for your blank, and press. Most fabric transfers run around 190–205°C for 45–60 seconds; mugs and hard blanks usually need longer.

  5. 5

    Peel and cool. Remove the transfer paper once it's cooled slightly, and your design is now permanent.

Quickstart

  • 1

    Turn on the press and let it reach temperature before pressing anything.

  • 2

    Check the settings for your specific blank type — temperature and time both matter, and vary by material.

  • 3

    Position your sublimation print face-down on the blank and secure it with heat-resistant tape so it can't shift.

  • 4

    Press for the full set time without opening early — lifting the press mid-cycle ruins the transfer.

  • 5

    Remove carefully — the press and the blank will both be very hot.

⚠ Safety & who to ask

  • The plates run at ~190–205°C — never touch the heated plates directly, and use the handle only.
  • Keep fingers well clear when closing the press.
  • Use heat-resistant gloves or a cloth when repositioning a hot blank.
  • Only press sublimation-rated/coated blanks — other materials can melt, scorch, or release fumes.
  • Let the press cool before leaving it unattended for the day.

First time pressing? Ask staff to run through timing/temperature for your specific blank first.
