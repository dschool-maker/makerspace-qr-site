Finishing

Spray Booth

A ventilated room for spray paint, primer, and sealants, with an extractor fan to pull fumes and overspray out of the space.

Idea → finished product

  1. 1

    Prep your piece. Sand, clean, and mask off anything you don't want painted before you bring it into the booth.

  2. 2

    Turn on the extractor and let it run before you start spraying — not after.

  3. 3

    Spray in light, even coats from about 20–30cm away, keeping the can/gun moving. Thin coats dry faster and drip less than one thick coat.

  4. 4

    Let it cure in the booth with the extractor still running, then move it to a safe drying spot if the next person needs the booth.

Quickstart

  • 1

    Check the booth is free and book/sign up if the space has a booking system.

  • 2

    Lay down protective sheeting for anything you don't want overspray landing on.

  • 3

    Switch on the extractor fan and confirm it's actually pulling air (check the airflow indicator or feel for draw at the vent).

  • 4

    Put on a respirator/mask rated for spray fumes — a dust mask alone isn't enough.

  • 5

    Spray, let cure with the extractor still on, then clean the booth for the next person.

⚠ Safety & who to ask

  • Always run the extractor while spraying and for a few minutes after you finish.
  • Wear a proper respirator rated for solvent/paint fumes, not just a dust mask.
  • No open flames or sparks near the booth — many spray products are flammable.
  • Don't spray directly at the extractor filter from close range; it clogs fast and needs replacing more often.
  • Clean up overspray and dispose of empty cans/rags properly before you leave.

First time using the booth? Ask staff to show you where the extractor switch and respirators are.
