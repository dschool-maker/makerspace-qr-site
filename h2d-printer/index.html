3D Printing

Bambu Lab H2D

A multi-material FDM 3D printer that can also mount a laser/cutting toolhead. If it's your first time here, start with plain FDM printing before touching the laser module.

Idea → finished product

  1. 1

    Find or design a model. Browse Bambu's MakerWorld for a ready-made design, or design your own in free CAD software (Tinkercad for beginners, Fusion 360 for more control). Export as an STL.

  2. 2

    Slice it. Open Bambu Studio, import the STL, choose your filament and the H2D printer profile, and slice. Bambu Studio shows you the print time and material use before you commit.

  3. 3

    Send & print. Send the sliced file to the printer over the network (or export to a USB drive). Watch the first layer — that's when most problems show up.

  4. 4

    Finish. Remove supports, sand rough edges, and it's done. For multi-color or multi-material jobs, load your extra filaments into the AMS before slicing.

Quickstart

  • 1

    Power on the printer and let it finish its startup checks.

  • 2

    Load your filament into the AMS (or directly, if not using AMS) and let the printer detect it.

  • 3

    On your laptop, open Bambu Studio, connect to this printer, and import your sliced or downloaded model.

  • 4

    Hit slice, double check the preview, then send the print.

  • 5

    Stay for the first layer. Once it's laying down cleanly, you're free to walk away — but check back periodically.

⚠ Safety & who to ask

  • Never touch the nozzle or heated bed while printing or right after — both run well above 100°C.
  • Keep hands clear of the toolhead and AMS while they're moving.
  • If you're switching to the laser/cutting module, read the H2D Laser Safety Document first and get a staff sign-off — different rules apply (ventilation, eyewear, no unattended laser jobs).
  • Don't leave the first layer of a print unattended — that's when failures happen.
  • Report any unusual smells, sounds, or filament jams to staff rather than troubleshooting live prints yourself.

Not sure about something? Ask a makerspace staff member on shift before you press print.
